Unit 2 Coverage Audit — What Krugman Modules 2 and 10–15 Do Not Cover

A complete audit of AP Macroeconomics Unit 2, Economic Indicators and the Business Cycle, against the Krugman modules that serve it. Every one of the unit's 26 essential knowledge statements is checked and classified. The question is narrow and literal: if a student reads only these modules, what Unit 2 requirements have they never met? A second question follows from the way this course was built: how many of those gaps do the published student guides already close?

Unit 2 is the reverse of Micro Unit 1. There, whole concepts were missing and needed other modules. Here the content is almost all on the page and the framework's words are not: the three GDP approach names, nonmarket, substitution bias and trough occur a combined 0 times in the unit's modules, and all but nonmarket occur 0 times in the whole book. The three absent statements are all Topic 2.7, and all three are taught later in the book, in Unit 3's modules.

Every gap but one has already been supplied by a published student guide. The exception is the cause of business cycles (MEA-2.A.1), which is the subject of Unit 3.

Method

Each essential knowledge statement was checked against the page ranges of the seven modules: Module 2, pp. 10–15, and Modules 10–15, pp. 102–147. The Section 3 review, pp. 148–157, is excluded: it covers the same modules and adds no new content. Presence or absence of the CED's key terms was verified by full-text search of all 927 sheets, not by reading impression.

Topic 2.7 is the weak topic, for a structural reason: its book module, Module 2, is a six-page introduction to the whole of macroeconomics written before the AD–AS model is available, and three of its five statements use concepts — potential output, the output gap — that the book introduces only with that model.

Remediation, in priority order

Most of this is already done. The list records what closes each gap and what is left.

2. Still open — gap 8, the cause of business cycles

MEA-2.A.1 says cycles happen "because of changes in aggregate supply and/or aggregate demand". No Unit 2 guide says so, and aggregate demand occurs 0 times in the unit modules. The fix is one sentence in the Module 2 lesson — cycles are caused by shifts in aggregate demand or aggregate supply, which Unit 3 models — and a forward link from the Module 2 guide's business-cycle entry. It costs two minutes, and the full treatment is Modules 17–19, the next unit.

3. Teach the surplus as background

In Module 14 especially: give the named costs five minutes, not the period, and spend the period on unexpected inflation and who it moves wealth between. The Module 14 notes give a one-period plan.

4. Treat full employment as one idea across three modules

Gaps 5, 6, 7 and 9 are supplied in two different guides (Modules 2 and 13). In class, connect them explicitly: potential output, full-employment output, the natural rate and zero cyclical unemployment are the same point, and the output gap and cyclical unemployment move in opposite directions. This is what sample FRQ 1 tests.
