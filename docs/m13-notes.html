The headline. This module is well aligned. All four essential knowledge statements of MEA-1.E are taught — the three types, the natural rate as frictional plus structural, cyclical unemployment as the deviation, and labor force characteristics as a reason the natural rate moves — with two printed equations and a review that drills the classification. Two things are missing, and both are the half of the framework that faces Unit 3: the CED defines the natural rate as the rate "when the economy produces full-employment real output", and full employment occurs 0 times in the module; and the module never shows the actual rate below the natural rate, which is exactly where the CED's own sample FRQ 1 starts (actual 5%, natural 6%). Section 3 supplies both.

Krugman's two stated objectives (p. 126):

  1. The three different types of unemployment and their causes
  2. The factors that determine the natural rate of unemployment

The first is MEA-1.E.1 and LO MEA-1.E b; the second is MEA-1.E.2 and MEA-1.E.4. Nothing in the module is off the framework's topic.

The essential knowledge statements, verbatim (CED p. 49):

MEA-1.E.1 — Economists primarily focus on three types of unemployment: cyclical, frictional, and structural. MEA-1.E.2 — The natural rate of unemployment is the unemployment rate that would exist when the economy produces full-employment real output. It is equal to the sum of frictional and structural unemployment. MEA-1.E.3 — The deviation of the actual unemployment rate from the natural rate is cyclical unemployment. MEA-1.E.4 — The natural rate of unemployment can gradually change over time because of such things as changes in labor force characteristics.

Two features of that wording matter.

The CED defines the natural rate by output; Krugman defines it by fluctuation. Krugman (p. 130): "the normal unemployment rate around which the actual unemployment rate fluctuates". The CED: the rate "when the economy produces full-employment real output". Both then say frictional plus structural. The CED's first clause is the bridge to Unit 3 — MEA-2.A.5 (p. 53) repeats it from the other side: potential output "is the level of GDP where unemployment is equal to the natural rate of unemployment". Krugman makes that link only in Module 33 (p. 328), 200 pages later.

"Primarily focus on three types" closes the list. The CED names three and no others. Seasonal occurs 0 times in the CED and 0 times in the module, but 13 times across the 21 papers in Practice Exams/, most of them review-book items. Teach it as a kind of frictional unemployment and as background (entry 17).

The arithmetic

Two equations (p. 130), and nothing in the module computes with them:

(13-1) Natural unemployment = Frictional unemployment + Structural unemployment (13-2) Actual unemployment = Natural unemployment + Cyclical unemployment

So cyclical = actual − natural. Every calculation item on this topic is that subtraction, run either way: frictional 3% and structural 2% give a natural rate of 5%; an actual rate of 8% then means cyclical unemployment of 3 points, and an actual rate of 4% means −1 point. The second case is the one the module never shows — see section 3.

Krugman's labor market (Figure 13.2) has no numbers. The student guide's entry 6 gives it some, on linear curves recomputed by build/check_m13_student.py: labor demand Q<sub>D</sub> = 300 − 10W, labor supply Q<sub>S</sub> = 20W (thousands of workers, dollars an hour). Equilibrium is $10 and 200,000 workers. A floor of $12 brings 240,000 job-seekers and 180,000 jobs: 60,000 structurally unemployed, and 20,000 fewer people working than with no floor — the two facts the text draws from the figure (p. 129).

5. Corrections to make explicitly in class

Krugman's definition of structural unemployment also fits a recession

The margin definition (p. 128) is "more people seeking jobs in a labor market than there are jobs available at the current wage rate". In a recession that is also true of most labor markets. What separates the two is not the surplus but its source and persistence: structural unemployment is a surplus in a particular labor market — a skill, an industry, a region, or a wage held above equilibrium — that persists even when the economy is at full employment; cyclical unemployment is an economy-wide surplus caused by output falling below its full-employment level, and it disappears when output recovers. Review MCQ 3 depends on the narrow reading; accept its key, and teach the test students will need on a stem: would this person be unemployed if the economy were at full employment? Yes → frictional or structural. No → cyclical.

"Natural" does not mean zero, constant, or good

Krugman says this himself (p. 130): the natural rate "changes over time, and it can be affected by economic policies". Students also need the third point: it is not zero. Full employment means unemployment equal to the natural rate — between 4.8% and 6.3% in the CBO estimates Krugman quotes (p. 131). Review MCQ 4 statement III is the test of it.

Cyclical unemployment can be negative

The CED says deviation; Krugman says difference. Both run both ways. With the natural rate at 6% and the actual rate at 5%, cyclical unemployment is −1 point and output is above full employment — the CED's sample FRQ 1. A student who has only ever seen cyclical unemployment as a positive number will call that economy "in recession". Rubrics reward the words "below the natural rate" and "above full-employment output"; teach those as the way to write it.

Unemployment benefits raise both kinds

The solution to CYU 3 attributes the rise in the natural rate to longer search — frictional unemployment. The text (pp. 130 and 132) says generous benefits "increase both structural and frictional unemployment". Either answer earns the point; the full one is better.

Seasonal is not a fourth type

Review books and teacher-made items use seasonal unemployment (13 hits across Practice Exams/). The CED names three types and only three. If a stem describes a lifeguard out of work every winter, the framework answer is frictional — and say so, rather than letting a fourth category appear.

Do not carry the Micro correction across

In AP Microeconomics unemployment is off-syllabus and the minimum wage is a price floor in Topic 2.8. Here the minimum wage is one cause of structural unemployment and the unemployment types are the topic. Frictional, structural, cyclical and natural rate each occur 0 times in the Micro CED.

Teach as a pair, never singly.

  • Frictional / structural — both present at full employment; one is search, the other a surplus
  • Structural / cyclical — both a surplus of job-seekers; only one survives full employment
  • Natural rate / actual rate — the gap between them is cyclical unemployment
  • Above / below the natural rate — recessionary and inflationary, positive and negative cyclical
  • A change in the actual rate / a change in the natural rate — the cycle moves the first, labor

force characteristics and policy move the second
