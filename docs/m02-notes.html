Verdict on alignment

This is an orientation module, not a delivery module. It names, in six pages, almost every measurement Unit 2 will later define precisely — and defines none of them to the standard the framework requires. Of Topic 2.7's five essential knowledge statements it delivers two (MEA-2.A.1 in part, MEA-2.A.2 in full) and omits three. Every other topic it touches is delivered properly by Modules 10–15.

Teach it as one period of vocabulary and orientation, not as coverage of any topic. Its real value is that a student who has read it knows what the rest of the unit is for. Its real risk is a teacher treating "we did unemployment in Module 2" as coverage of Topic 2.3.

The EK statements, in the CED's own words

MEA-2.A.1 — "Business cycles are fluctuations in aggregate output and employment because of changes in aggregate supply and/or aggregate demand." (CED p. 53)
MEA-2.A.2 — "The phases of a business cycle are recession and expansion." (CED p. 53)
MEA-1.C.1 — "The unemployment rate is the percentage of the labor force that is out of work." (CED p. 48)

Krugman's unemployment-rate definition and the CED's are the same statement in different words — "that is unemployed" against "that is out of work". Either wording earns the point.

MEA-2.A.1 is delivered only in part. Krugman gives the fluctuations; he does not give the cause. The words aggregate demand and aggregate supply appear 0 times in Module 2 (357 and 275 times in the book as a whole, from Module 17 onwards). Say aloud that the cause is coming, or students will carry "cycles just happen" into Unit 3.

Peak and trough are absent from the whole book

The word trough appears zero times in all 45 modules. Peak appears 27 times, every one of them in ordinary language — "demand for gasoline peaks in the summer", "peak earnings", "fallen from their peak" — and never as the turning point of a business cycle.

MEA-2.A.3 is one sentence: "The turning points of a business cycle are peak and trough." A student who learns the cycle from this textbook alone cannot label a diagram the exam asks them to label. This is the single most important thing to add to Module 2, and it takes four minutes.

On the costs of inflation. Krugman gives two costs in Module 2: inflation discourages people from holding cash, and deflation makes holding cash more attractive than investing (pp. 12–13). Both are real and both are worth teaching. Neither is MEA-1.H.1, which is the only cost of inflation the framework states: "Unexpected inflation arbitrarily redistributes wealth from one group of individuals to another group, such as lenders to borrowers" (CED p. 51). The phrase unexpected inflation appears 3 times in the whole book and 0 times in Module 2. Do not let "we covered the costs of inflation" stand after this module.

5. Corrections to make explicitly in class

1. The Micro vocabulary correction is now reversed. If your students took AP Microeconomics, they were trained off unemployment and recession toward the Micro framework's underutilised resources. In Macro both words are examinable content — unemployment is Topic 2.3, business cycles are Topic 2.7. Say this out loud in the first five minutes. A student who has been corrected once will hedge, and hedging costs points on a definition item.

2. Add peak and trough. Four minutes, on the board, with Figure 2.1 open. Phases are periods; turning points are moments. See the red box in section 3.

3. Give the unemployment rate its denominator. Krugman's definition is correct and students still divide by the population. Write unemployment rate = unemployed ÷ labor force × 100 and do one number. Then add the participation rate — labor force ÷ adult population × 100 — which is MEA-1.C.2 and is not in this module at all.

4. MCQ 4's key is the textbook's cost of inflation, not the framework's. Krugman's answer — people are discouraged from holding cash — is right and is not what MEA-1.H.1 says. Use the item, then add one sentence: the cost the exam asks about is that unexpected inflation redistributes wealth from lenders to borrowers.

5. Expansion is not economic growth. FRQ 1 is built on it and students lose the third point by defining both terms correctly and never stating the difference. Drill the sentence: an expansion raises actual output until the next downturn; growth raises what the economy is capable of producing.

6. "Recovery" is a trap late in an expansion. Krugman gives it as a synonym. It stops describing anything once output passes its previous peak.

Teaching order — what depends on what

Six of the module's sixteen concept entries carry a hard prerequisite. Each row means the left entry has to be in place before the right one makes sense. (Section 8 splits some of these into their separate glossary words — employment, unemployment and labor force are one entry but three lines there.)

Teachbeforebecause
Employment, unemployment and the labor forceUnemployment rateit supplies both the numerator and — the part students get wrong — the denominator
Employment, unemployment and the labor forceLabor force participation rateit supplies the numerator
Aggregate outputPotential output and the output gapthe gap is the difference from it
Unemployment ratePotential output and the output gappotential output is defined at the natural rate of unemployment
Aggregate outputReal GDP per capitathe same quantity, deflated and divided by population
Inflation and deflationReal GDP per capita"real" is exactly the price change being removed

The minute-by-minute table above already runs in this order, with one exception worth watching: potential output and the output gap depends on both aggregate output and the unemployment rate, so it cannot be introduced during the Figure 2.1 segment even though the diagram invites it.

Teach the cycle before its parts. Recession, expansion, peak and trough, and the output gap are all components of the business cycle, not free-standing terms. Name the cycle first and hang the four on it; introduced separately they become four definitions to memorise.

Teach as a pair, never singly. Each of these is defined by its contrast:

  • Recession / expansion — the same two variables moving in opposite directions
  • Expansion / economic growth — the module's central discrimination, and the one FRQ 1 tests
  • Business cycle / economic growth — short-run fluctuation against long-run capacity
  • Unemployment rate / labor force participation rate — same inputs, different denominator
  • Inflation and deflation / price stability — the two departures against the state itself
