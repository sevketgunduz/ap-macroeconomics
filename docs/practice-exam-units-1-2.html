1.A coastal town has an unlimited supply of seawater but only 40 hectares of land suitable for building. Which of the following best explains why the town still faces scarcity?

Answer: (C). Scarcity is a relationship between a resource and the demands placed on it, not a statement that a resource has run out. Land is scarce because the town has more uses for it than hectares. Note the framework's wording: most resources are scarce - seawater here is the exception that makes the point.

2.Which of the following is the best statement of why individuals and societies must make choices?

Answer: (B). This is MOD-1.A.1 almost word for word. The binding constraint is resources, not money and not policy: money is a claim on resources, and even an economy with no government would still have to choose.

3.An economy produces only wheat and steel and is currently operating at a point inside its production possibilities curve. Which of the following must be true?

Answer: (D). A point inside the curve means resources are underutilised - unemployed or idle - so more of both goods is attainable without any new resources. Only on the curve does more of one require less of the other.

4.The table shows four attainable, efficient combinations of boats and bicycles that the country of Teloria can produce. What is the opportunity cost of increasing boat production from 2 boats to 3 boats?

Answer: (A). Read the two rows: at 2 boats the economy makes 70 bicycles, at 3 boats it makes 40. The opportunity cost of the third boat is the 30 bicycles given up. Note also that the cost per boat rises as more boats are made - 10, then 20, then 30 - which is increasing opportunity cost, and it is why a PPC bows outward.

5.A production possibilities curve is drawn as a straight line rather than a bowed-out curve. What does the straight line indicate?

Answer: (E). The shape of the PPC is determined by what happens to opportunity cost. A straight line has a constant slope, so the trade-off never changes; a bowed-out curve means increasing opportunity cost, because resources are not equally suited to both goods.

6.A country discovers a large new deposit of iron ore and, in the same year, a new technology doubles the output of every factory. What happens to the country's production possibilities curve?

Answer: (B). Economic growth is an outward shift of the PPC, and the framework names both of its causes: a change in the quantity of resources and a change in technology. Both happened here, so the whole curve moves out.

7.During a severe recession, a country's factories run at half their normal capacity and many workers are without jobs. On a production possibilities curve, this situation is best represented by

Answer: (C). Idle factories and unemployed workers are the definition of underutilised resources, which is a point inside the curve. The curve itself has not moved - the country's capacity is unchanged, it is simply not being used.

8.Country X can produce more of every good than Country Y using the same quantity of resources. Which of the following is true?

Answer: (D). Absolute advantage is about producing more with the same resources; comparative advantage is about producing at a lower opportunity cost. As long as the two countries' opportunity costs differ, each has a comparative advantage in something - which is why a country that is worse at everything can still gain from trade.

9.The table shows the maximum output each country can produce in one day with its available resources. Which country has a comparative advantage in cloth, and what is that country's opportunity cost of one unit of cloth?

Answer: (B). Work in opportunity costs, not totals. For Alta, 40 cloth or 80 grain, so one cloth costs 80/40 = 2 grain. For Bray, 20 cloth or 10 grain, so one cloth costs 10/20 = 1/2 grain. Bray gives up less grain per unit of cloth, so Bray has the comparative advantage even though Alta can produce more of both goods.

10.Using the same two countries, Alta's opportunity cost of one unit of cloth is 2 units of grain and Bray's is 1/2 unit of grain. Which of the following terms of trade would be accepted by both countries?

Answer: (A). Mutually beneficial terms of trade must lie strictly between the two opportunity costs - here between 1/2 and 2 grain per cloth. Only 1 grain per cloth sits inside that range. Note that 2 grain is exactly Alta's own cost, so Alta would gain nothing, and the boundary cases are never 'accepted by both'.

11.Two countries specialise according to comparative advantage and then trade. Which of the following best explains the resulting gain?

Answer: (E). Specialisation moves production to the lower-cost producer, and trade then lets each country consume outside its own PPC. Note what does not happen: neither country's PPC shifts - production possibilities are unchanged, only consumption possibilities expand.

12.A country decides to produce everything it consumes rather than trading with its neighbours. Compared with specialising according to comparative advantage and trading, this decision will most likely

Answer: (C). Self-sufficiency forces the country back onto its own PPC. Nothing about its resources or technology has changed, so the curve does not move - but the consumption points beyond it are no longer available.

13.The price of a good falls from $8 to $6 and the quantity that buyers purchase rises from 100 to 150 units per week. Nothing else changes. This is best described as

Answer: (B). The good's own price changed, so buyers moved along an unchanged demand curve: quantity demanded rose. Demand - the whole curve - has not changed. This distinction is the most frequently tested idea in Topic 1.4.

14.Consumer incomes in a country rise sharply. In the market for a normal good, which of the following explains what happens to the market demand curve and why?

Answer: (A). Income is the determinant the framework names in MKT-2.B.1, and for a normal good a rise in income increases demand at every price. Option C is the trap: a determinant does not need to be the good's own price to move the curve - in fact the own price is the one thing that does not shift it.

15.Consumers widely expect the price of a durable good to fall sharply next month. What is the most likely effect on the demand for that good today?

Answer: (D). Expectations act on today's curve. Expecting a lower price later makes waiting worthwhile, so less is bought at every price today and the curve shifts left. Nothing has happened to today's price, so option B cannot be right.

16.The wage paid to workers who assemble furniture rises sharply. In the market for furniture, this will

Answer: (C). Wages are an input price - the determinant MKT-2.D.1 names. A higher input price makes production more costly, so less is offered at every price and the supply curve shifts left. Option E repeats the own-price confusion from the demand side.

17.Which of the following best explains why the market supply curve for a good slopes upward?

Answer: (E). The law of supply is a positive relationship between price and quantity supplied: as the price rises, it becomes worth bringing less productive resources into use. Option A describes a preference rather than a relationship between two variables, which is what the law states.

18.A competitive market is in equilibrium. Which of the following must be true at the equilibrium price?

Answer: (D). Equilibrium is defined by one condition: quantity demanded equals quantity supplied, at one price. Option A is false - only buyers willing and able to pay that price buy - and option E confuses equality at one price with equality everywhere.

Answer: (A). At $6 the quantity supplied is 90 and the quantity demanded is 50, and $6 is above the equilibrium price of $4 (where both are 70). Surplus = quantity supplied − quantity demanded = 90 − 50 = 40 units. Subtracting in the other order gives a negative number and the wrong name.

20.In the market for a good, consumer incomes rise and at the same time the price of a key input falls. Which of the following describes the effect on the equilibrium price and the equilibrium quantity?

Answer: (E). A rise in income shifts demand right; a fall in an input price shifts supply right. Both shifts push quantity up, so quantity certainly rises. They push price in opposite directions, so the price change is indeterminate without knowing which shift is larger. The rule: shifts in the same direction settle the quantity; shifts in opposite directions settle the price.

21.In the circular flow model of a closed economy with no government, which of the following correctly describes the product market?

Answer: (C). In the product market the direction is firms → goods and services → households, with money flowing the other way. Option A describes the factor market, where the roles are reversed - households own the resources.

22.Which of the following would be included in this year's gross domestic product of a country?

Answer: (B). GDP counts the market value of final goods and services produced within the period. A haircut qualifies. The house was produced in an earlier year, stock purchases and gifts are transfers of ownership rather than production, and the flour is an intermediate good whose value is already inside the price of the bread.

24.Why does the expenditure approach to measuring gross domestic product exclude the value of intermediate goods?

Answer: (D). Double-counting is the whole reason. The steel in a car is paid for once by the car manufacturer and again by the car buyer; only the final sale is counted, which is why GDP measures final output.

25.A country's firms produce $50 billion more output this year, but households save all of the resulting extra income rather than spending it. Which of the following is true of the income and expenditure approaches to measuring GDP in that year?

Answer: (E). The two approaches are equal by construction: every dollar of spending is a dollar of someone's income. Output that is produced but not sold is counted as inventory investment, which keeps the expenditure side in step with the income side.

26.Which of the following best describes the relationship between the expenditure approach and the income approach to measuring gross domestic product?

Answer: (B). The circular flow is the reason: spending by one party is income to another, so adding up all the spending and adding up all the income must reach the same total. Transfer payments are excluded from both, because nothing was produced.

27.Two countries have identical levels of real GDP per capita. Which of the following best explains why this may be a poor guide to how their standards of living compare?

Answer: (A). This is the content of MEA-1.B.1. Unpaid household work, the underground economy, leisure, the distribution of income and the environmental cost of production are all invisible to GDP. Options C and E describe things GDP does not do.

28.In Country R, many people grow their own food and care for their own children at home. In Country S, people buy the same food in shops and pay for childcare. The two countries produce and consume similar amounts in real terms. Measured GDP will most likely

Answer: (C). GDP records market transactions. Identical real activity produces a higher measured GDP wherever more of it is bought and sold rather than done at home - one of the clearest limitations of GDP as a welfare measure.

29.A country's real GDP rises by 4% in a year. Which of the following can be concluded with certainty?

Answer: (D). Only the definition can be concluded. Option E fails unless the population is unchanged - if population grew faster than 4%, real GDP per capita fell. The others are exactly what GDP does not measure.

30.In official labour-market statistics, a person is counted as unemployed if that person is

Answer: (B). Three conditions: no job, available, and actively searching. Option E describes a discouraged worker, who is counted as not in the labour force. Option C describes underemployment, which the official rate does not capture, and benefits have nothing to do with the definition.

31.What is the unemployment rate in Dervane?

Answer: (C). The labour force is employed + unemployed = 144 + 16 = 160 million. The unemployment rate is unemployed ÷ labour force = 16 ÷ 160 = 10.0%. Dividing by the adult population instead (16 ÷ 250 = 6.4%) is the standard error - the denominator is the labour force, not the population.

32.What is the labour force participation rate in Dervane, and what does it measure?

Answer: (A). Participation rate = labour force ÷ adult population = 160 ÷ 250 = 64.0%. The numerator is the labour force, so it counts the unemployed as participating - they are looking. Option B has the right number with the wrong meaning, which is the distinction being tested.

33.A software engineer leaves one firm and spends six weeks interviewing before accepting a better-matched position at another firm. During those six weeks she is best classified as

Answer: (D). Frictional unemployment is the time taken to match workers to jobs. Her skills are in demand and the economy is not in recession - she is simply between jobs, which is the one kind of unemployment that exists even at full employment.

34.A coal region's mines close permanently as electricity generation shifts to other sources. Miners remain out of work for years because their skills do not match the jobs now available. This is an example of

Answer: (B). Structural unemployment is a mismatch between the skills workers have and the skills employers want, and it persists because retraining or relocating takes time. Like frictional unemployment, it is part of the natural rate.

35.During a deep recession, a country's unemployment rate rises from 5% to 9% while its natural rate of unemployment remains 5%. Which of the following explains the increase?

Answer: (E). Cyclical unemployment is the gap between the actual rate and the natural rate: 9 − 5 = 4 percentage points. The natural rate is made up of frictional and structural unemployment and is stated here to be unchanged.

36.After a long search, a large number of unemployed workers give up looking for work and stop searching. Holding everything else constant, this will cause the measured unemployment rate to

Answer: (A). Discouraged workers are no longer actively searching, so they leave both the numerator and the denominator. Removing a person from the unemployed and from the labour force lowers the ratio - which is why a falling unemployment rate can be bad news, and why the participation rate is read alongside it.

37.An economy is producing at its full-employment level of output. Which of the following is true of its unemployment rate?

Answer: (C). Full employment does not mean zero unemployment. At full-employment output, cyclical unemployment is zero and what remains is the natural rate - the frictional and structural unemployment that exists even in a healthy economy.

38.In a given year a country's actual unemployment rate is 3.5% while its natural rate is 5%. Which of the following best describes this economy?

Answer: (D). Cyclical unemployment = actual − natural = 3.5 − 5 = −1.5 percentage points. A negative cyclical rate means output is above the full-employment level - an inflationary gap. Students who assume unemployment can never be below the natural rate miss this case entirely.

39.The consumer price index is best described as a measure of

Answer: (E). Two features define the CPI: a fixed basket representing typical household purchases, and a comparison with a base year set to 100. Option A describes GDP, and option C would be closer to the GDP deflator, which covers all domestic output rather than a household basket.

40.A country's fixed market basket cost $250 in the base year and costs $300 this year. What is the value of the consumer price index this year?

Answer: (C). CPI = (cost of the basket this year ÷ cost in the base year) × 100 = (300 ÷ 250) × 100 = 120. The index is not a percentage change: prices have risen 20%, and the index that records that is 120, not 20.

41.A country's consumer price index was 150 in 2023 and 162 in 2024. What was the inflation rate in 2024?

Answer: (A). Inflation rate = (new index − old index) ÷ old index × 100 = (162 − 150) ÷ 150 × 100 = 8.0%. Subtracting the indices and stopping gives 12, which is a change in index points, not a rate.

42.The price of beef rises sharply and many households respond by buying more chicken instead. Because the consumer price index uses a fixed basket, this behaviour causes the index to

Answer: (B). This is substitution bias. The fixed basket assumes households keep buying the dearer good in the same quantity, so it charges them for a purchase they no longer make - and the measured rise in the cost of living is larger than the rise households actually experience.

43.Which of the following is a difference between the consumer price index and the GDP deflator?

Answer: (D). Coverage is the real difference, and it runs both ways: the CPI includes imported consumer goods but excludes capital goods and government purchases, while the deflator covers everything produced at home but nothing produced abroad. Option B has the two the wrong way round.

44.A statistical agency updates the fixed market basket used to calculate its consumer price index so that it reflects what households buy today. Compared with the old basket, the new index will most likely

Answer: (C). Updating the basket addresses substitution bias directly. It does not remove every bias - new-product and quality-change biases remain, which is why option D overstates the effect.

45.A country's price index rises from 200 to 206 in one year, having risen from 190 to 200 the year before. Which of the following best describes the two years?

Answer: (E). First year: (200 − 190) ÷ 190 = 5.26%. Second year: (206 − 200) ÷ 200 = 3.0%. A falling inflation rate is disinflation, not deflation - the price level is still rising, just more slowly. Deflation would require the index itself to fall.

46.Unexpected inflation occurs in an economy where many loans are made at fixed nominal interest rates. Which of the following describes the main effect the framework identifies?

Answer: (E). The examinable cost of unexpected inflation is redistribution. Borrowers repay in cheaper dollars and gain; lenders receive those dollars and lose. The nominal value of the debt is unchanged - it is its real value that falls, which is why option D is wrong.

47.A loan is made at a nominal interest rate of 5% when both parties expect inflation of 3%. Inflation actually turns out to be 6%. What is the actual real interest rate, and who benefits from the surprise?

Answer: (B). Actual real rate = nominal − actual inflation = 5 − 6 = −1%. The parties expected a real return of 5 − 3 = 2%; instead the lender receives a negative real return, so the borrower gains at the lender's expense. A negative real rate means the lender gets back less purchasing power than was lent.

48.Inflation over the next three years turns out to be much higher than anyone expected. Which of the following groups is most likely to benefit?

Answer: (D). Unexpected inflation transfers purchasing power from those owed fixed nominal amounts to those who owe them. The mortgage holder repays a fixed nominal sum with cheaper dollars. Everyone else listed is receiving a fixed nominal amount, so all of them lose.

49.A country's inflation has been steady at 4% for many years and everyone expects it to continue. Compared with inflation that arrives unexpectedly, fully anticipated inflation

Answer: (C). If inflation is expected, lenders build it into the nominal interest rate and workers negotiate for it in wage contracts, so the real outcomes are close to what both sides intended. It is the surprise that redistributes.

50.A country's nominal GDP rises by 7% in a year while its price level rises by 7%. What happened to real GDP?

Answer: (E). Nominal GDP changes for two reasons: output and prices. If the entire 7% rise is accounted for by prices, no additional goods and services were produced, so real GDP is roughly unchanged. This is exactly why real GDP, not nominal, is used to judge economic performance.

51.In 2024 a country's nominal GDP was $660 billion and its GDP deflator was 110 (base year 2020 = 100). What was its real GDP in 2024, measured in 2020 dollars?

Answer: (A). Real GDP = (nominal GDP ÷ deflator) × 100 = (660 ÷ 110) × 100 = $600 billion. Because the deflator is above 100, prices have risen since the base year, so real GDP must be below nominal GDP - which rules out $726 billion immediately.

52.A country's nominal GDP is $840 billion and its real GDP is $700 billion. What is the GDP deflator?

Answer: (C). GDP deflator = (nominal GDP ÷ real GDP) × 100 = (840 ÷ 700) × 100 = 120. Prices are 20% above the base year. Option A inverts the ratio and option E forgets to multiply by 100.

53.Which of the following best explains why real GDP is preferred to nominal GDP when comparing a country's output across years?

Answer: (B). Holding prices at base-year levels strips out price changes and leaves output. Real GDP is not always larger - it is smaller than nominal whenever the deflator is above 100, and larger whenever it is below.

54.In the base year of a country's GDP deflator, which of the following must be true?

Answer: (D). In the base year the index is set to 100, so dividing nominal GDP by 100 and multiplying by 100 leaves it unchanged. Real and nominal GDP are equal in the base year, and only there.

55.Country P's real GDP grows 3% while its population grows 4%. Country Q's real GDP grows 1% while its population is unchanged. Which country's real GDP per capita grew more?

Answer: (A). Real GDP per capita is real GDP divided by population. In Country P output per person falls by roughly 1%, because the population grew faster than output; in Country Q it rises by 1%. Faster total growth does not mean rising living standards.

56.The business cycle is best described as

Answer: (B). Two variables define the cycle - real output and employment - and they move together. Option A describes long-run economic growth, which is a different idea and is tested separately.

57.A country's real output has been falling for several quarters and then begins to rise again. The turning point at which the decline ends and the recovery begins is called

Answer: (E). A trough is the bottom turning point, where a recession ends and an expansion begins; a peak is the top, where an expansion ends. Expansion and recession are the phases between the turning points, not the turning points themselves.

58.An economy's actual real output is below its potential output. Which of the following describes this economy?

Answer: (E). Output below potential is a negative output gap, and the two measures move together: when output is below potential, unemployment is above the natural rate, because cyclical unemployment is positive.

59.Which of the following distinguishes an expansion from long-run economic growth?

Answer: (D). The distinction is between moving towards capacity and raising capacity. An expansion closes a negative output gap; growth shifts potential output upward. Confusing them is the most common error in Topic 2.7.

60.During a recession, which of the following combinations would most likely be observed?

Answer: (A). The three move together in a recession: output falls below potential, giving a negative output gap, and firms lay workers off, so unemployment rises above the natural rate. Options B and C pair the variables in directions that cannot occur together.
