Types of Unemployment

Seventeen terms from Module 13 of Krugman’s Economics for AP: why the unemployment rate is never zero, the three types of unemployment, and the natural rate they add up to. Two equations, one classification skill, and two ideas your textbook leaves for later — what full employment means, and what happens when unemployment falls below its natural rate.

The 17 terms

01

Job creation and job destruction

○ Background
Definition

Even in good times, jobs are constantly being created and destroyed as industries rise and fall and firms succeed or fail.

Textbook pages 126–127.

Unemployment never reaches zero because the economy never stands still. New technologies and changing tastes close some jobs and open others; some firms are badly run or unlucky. Workers also quit, to move, to change careers or because they are unhappy.

Each of those changes leaves someone looking for work for a while. That churning is where the first type of unemployment comes from.

Watch out. A low unemployment rate does not mean nobody is losing a job. It means people find new ones quickly.

Textbook

July 2007. The unemployment rate was only 4.7%, yet 4.5 million job separations — people fired or quitting — happened in that one month.

Numeric

A half-century low. Over the past fifty years the U.S. rate has never fallen below 2.9%.

Illustrative

Two industries at once. Telecom jobs slumped after 2000 while healthcare jobs surged with new treatments and an ageing population.

How it connects. Churning is why there is always job search (entry 2), and so always frictional unemployment (entry 3).

Check yourself

1.1 If jobs are plentiful, why do millions still lose jobs each month?

Show answer

Because jobs are always being destroyed in some firms and industries while others are created.

1.2 Is job destruction always a sign of a weak economy?

Show answer

No. It happens in booms too, as some industries shrink and others grow.

02

Job search

★ Exam
Definition

The time workers spend looking for a job that fits their skills and pays accordingly.

Textbook page 127, margin.

If all jobs and all workers were alike, nobody would need to search. They are not, and information about them is not perfect, so finding the right match takes time — usually at least a few weeks.

Searching is often the sensible thing to do. A laid-off programmer could take the first clerical job advertised; she does better, and so does the economy, if she waits for one that uses her skills.

Watch out. A worker who turns down poor offers while searching is still unemployed — and still frictionally unemployed, not lazy.

Textbook

The laid-off programmer. She passes over a clerical job to look for one that uses her programming skills.

Numeric

Short searches in good times. In 2000, 45% of the unemployed had been out of work for less than 5 weeks.

Illustrative

A chef who moved. She relocates with her family and spends a month visiting restaurants in the new city before accepting an offer.

How it connects. It comes from job creation and destruction (entry 1), and the time it takes is frictional unemployment (entry 3).

Check yourself

2.1 Why does job search take time even when jobs are plentiful?

Show answer

Because workers and jobs differ, and finding a good match takes information and time.

2.2 Is it a waste for a skilled worker to turn down a low-skill job while searching?

Show answer

No. A better match makes the worker and the economy more productive.

03

Frictional unemployment

★ Exam
Definition

Unemployment due to the time workers spend in job search.

Textbook page 127, margin.

Some frictional unemployment is inevitable, for two reasons: jobs are always being created and destroyed, and new workers are always entering the labor force — school-leavers, graduates, and people coming back after time away.

A limited amount is harmless and even good: the economy is more productive when workers take the time to find well-matched jobs, and short spells of search cause little hardship. It exists even when there are as many jobs as job-seekers.

Watch out. Frictional unemployment is part of the natural rate. It does not disappear at full employment.

Textbook

January 2010. Of 14.8 million unemployed, 1.2 million were new entrants and 3.6 million re-entrants — 32.4% of the unemployed were just joining or rejoining.

Numeric

The diagram above. Frictional unemployment is 3% of the labor force at the start — three-fifths of all unemployment.

Illustrative

A new graduate. She finished her degree in May and is interviewing for her first job. Frictionally unemployed.

How it connects. It is the time spent in job search (entry 2), caused by churning (entry 1). Its share changes over the cycle (entry 4); it is half of the natural rate (entry 9); benefits (entry 8) lengthen it; entry 14 tells it from the other types; seasonal unemployment (entry 17) is a kind of it.

Check yourself

3.1 Name the two reasons frictional unemployment is always present.

Show answer

Jobs are constantly created and destroyed, and new workers are always entering the labor force.

3.2 Does an economy with no frictional unemployment sound healthy?

Show answer

No. It would mean nobody ever changes jobs or enters the labor force.

04

Frictional unemployment over the cycle

★ Exam
Definition

When the unemployment rate is low, spells are short and most unemployment is frictional. When the rate is high, frictional unemployment is a smaller share.

Textbook pages 127–128, Figure 13.1.

Frictional unemployment does not vanish in a slump — people still change jobs and enter the labor force. But other unemployment piles on top of it, and people stay out of work longer, so its share of the total falls.

Figure 13.1 shows it through how long people have been unemployed. Short spells are mostly search; very long ones are mostly something else.

Watch out. The share falls in a recession; the amount need not. Read which one the question asks about.

Textbook

2000 against 2010. In 2000, 45% had been unemployed under 5 weeks and 11% for 27 weeks or more. In early 2010 it was 20% and 41%.

Numeric

The diagram above. At the start frictional unemployment is 60% of all unemployment. Press A recession: the same 3% is now 37.5% of the total.

Illustrative

A town in two years. In a good year most of its unemployed are students and people between jobs; after a factory closes, most are laid-off workers who have searched for months.

How it connects. It is about frictional unemployment (entry 3) and what cyclical unemployment (entry 11) adds on top of it.

Check yourself

4.1 In 2000, what share of the unemployed had been out of work less than 15 weeks?

Show answer

45% + 32% = 77%.

4.2 Why is frictional unemployment a larger share when the rate is low?

Show answer

Because the other kinds have shrunk, so search makes up most of what is left.

05

Structural unemployment

★ Exam
Definition

Unemployment that results when there are more people seeking jobs in a labor market than there are jobs available at the current wage — a persistent surplus of workers with a particular skill or in a particular place.

Textbook page 128, margin.

Frictional unemployment exists even when job-seekers and jobs are equal in number. Structural unemployment is different: there are simply too many workers for the jobs in one labor market. Sometimes their skills are no longer wanted; sometimes there are more of them in a region than there are jobs; sometimes the wage is held above the level that would clear the market.

It lasts. Searching longer will not fix it, because the jobs are not there for that skill, in that place, at that wage. And it is still there when the economy as a whole is doing well.

Watch out. A recession also leaves more job-seekers than jobs. What makes unemployment structural is that it would remain at full employment.

Textbook

Eastern Germany. After reunification, wages were set near western levels while productivity stayed far lower. In December 2009 unemployment was 12.1% in the east against 6.7% in the west.

Numeric

The labor market below. At a $12 wage, 240 thousand people want jobs and 180 thousand jobs exist: 60 thousand structurally unemployed.

Illustrative

Typesetters. Once publishers switched to software, typesetters’ skills were not wanted at any firm, however strong the economy.

How it connects. It is the other half of the natural rate (entry 9). One cause is the minimum wage (entry 6); others are unions and efficiency wages (entry 7). Entry 14 tells it from cyclical unemployment.

Check yourself

5.1 Coal mines in a region close for good. The miners cannot find work there. Which type?

Show answer

Structural. Their skills and location no longer match the jobs available.

5.2 Can structural unemployment exist when the economy is booming?

Show answer

Yes. It is a mismatch in particular labor markets, not a shortage of output overall.

The labor market behind structural unemployment

Your textbook’s Figure 13.2, with numbers. Labor demand is Q = 300 − 10W and labor supply is Q = 20W, in thousands of workers at a wage of W dollars an hour. They meet at $10 and 200 thousand workers. A minimum wage of $12 is above that.

06

Minimum wages

★ Exam
Definition

A binding minimum wage — one set above the equilibrium wage — makes the quantity of labor supplied exceed the quantity demanded. The surplus is structural unemployment.

Textbook pages 128–129, Figure 13.2.

At the equilibrium wage the number of jobs equals the number of people who want them, and there is no structural unemployment — only frictional. A legal floor above that wage does two things at once: more people want to work, and firms want fewer workers.

So there are more job-seekers than jobs, and fewer people working than with no minimum wage. For most workers the minimum wage is below what they earn and does nothing; it binds for the less skilled.

Watch out. A minimum wage below the equilibrium wage is not binding and causes no structural unemployment.

Textbook

The 2009 floor. The national minimum wage was $7.25 an hour — irrelevant for most workers, binding for some less-skilled ones.

Numeric

The labor market below. Equilibrium is $10 and 200 thousand workers. At $12, 240 thousand look for work and 180 thousand are hired: 60 thousand unemployed, and 20 thousand fewer working than with no floor.

Illustrative

A higher floor. Raise the floor in the diagram above and structural unemployment rises, so the natural rate rises with it — press A higher minimum wage.

How it connects. It is one cause of structural unemployment (entry 5). Unions and efficiency wages (entry 7) work the same way, and combined with new technology it can raise the natural rate — entry 16.

Check yourself

6.1 Labor demand Q = 300 − 10W, labor supply Q = 20W. What happens with a floor of $11?

Show answer

Supplied 220, demanded 190: 30 thousand structurally unemployed.

6.2 A floor of $8 in the same market. How much structural unemployment does it cause?

Show answer

None. $8 is below the $10 equilibrium, so the floor does not bind.

07

Labor unions and efficiency wages

○ Background
Definition

Two more reasons a wage can sit above equilibrium: unions bargaining collectively, and firms paying efficiency wages — above-market pay to make workers work harder.

Textbook pages 129–130, margin.

Unions win higher wages and benefits by bargaining for all of a firm’s workers together, backed by the threat of a strike. Some firms raise pay on their own, because effort is hard to watch and a worker paid above the market works hard to keep the job.

Either way the effect is the minimum-wage diagram again: more people want those jobs than there are jobs, so the result is structural unemployment.

Watch out. Your exam does not name these. Know the mechanism — a wage above equilibrium — and you can handle any version of it.

Textbook

Your textbook’s comparison. Union members earn higher wages and more generous benefits than non-union workers with similar skills.

Numeric

The labor market below. A union wage of $12 does exactly what the $12 floor does: 60 thousand more job-seekers than jobs.

Illustrative

A delivery firm. It pays drivers well above the going rate so that none risks being fired for slacking. Hundreds apply for every opening.

How it connects. They are the minimum-wage mechanism (entry 6) with a different cause, and they lead to structural unemployment (entry 5).

Check yourself

7.1 Why does collective bargaining have the same effect as a minimum wage?

Show answer

Both push the wage above equilibrium, so more people want jobs than firms will fill.

7.2 Why might a firm choose to pay more than it has to?

Show answer

To make workers try harder to keep a job they could not easily match elsewhere.

08

Unemployment benefits

★ Exam
Definition

Payments to laid-off workers while they look for work. Generous benefits raise both frictional and structural unemployment, and so raise the natural rate.

Textbook pages 130 and 132.

Benefits help people through a job loss. Their side effect is that they lower the cost of staying unemployed, so people search longer and are slower to accept a job they would otherwise take.

The more generous and the longer-lasting the benefits, the bigger the effect. Generous European benefits are often blamed for Eurosclerosis, the persistently high unemployment in some European countries.

Watch out. More generous benefits raise the natural rate. They are not a cause of cyclical unemployment.

Textbook

The U.S. against Europe. U.S. benefits replace a small part of income and usually end after 26 weeks; in much of Europe they are larger and last longer.

Numeric

The diagram above. Press More generous benefits: frictional rises to 3.5%, structural to 2.5%, and the natural rate to 6%.

Illustrative

Two job offers. A laid-off accountant on generous benefits turns down two offers and keeps searching for a better one; without benefits she would have taken the first.

How it connects. They lengthen frictional unemployment (entry 3), raise the natural rate (entry 9), and are one of the policies of entry 16.

Check yourself

8.1 A country doubles how long unemployment benefits last. What happens to the natural rate?

Show answer

It rises. People search longer, so frictional and structural unemployment rise.

8.2 Does that change raise cyclical unemployment?

Show answer

No. It changes the natural rate, not the gap between the actual and natural rates.

09

Natural rate of unemployment

★ Exam
Definition

Frictional plus structural unemployment. The normal unemployment rate around which the actual rate fluctuates.

Textbook page 130, margin, and equation 13-1.

Because some frictional unemployment is inevitable and most economies also have some structural unemployment, a certain amount of unemployment is normal. That amount is the natural rate.

“Natural” does not mean zero, and it does not mean fixed. The natural rate changes over time and can be changed by policy — entries 15 and 16.

Watch out. Cyclical unemployment is not part of the natural rate. The natural rate is two types, not three.

Textbook

Estimates over time. The U.S. natural rate was about 5.3% in 1950, rose to 6.3% by the end of the 1970s and later fell to 4.8%.

Numeric

The diagram above. 3% frictional + 2% structural = a natural rate of 5%.

Illustrative

Counting people. A labor force of 160 million with 4.8 million frictionally and 3.2 million structurally unemployed: a natural rate of 5%.

How it connects. It adds frictional (entry 3) and structural (entry 5) unemployment. It is the rate at full employment (entry 10); the gap from it is cyclical unemployment (entry 11). Benefits (entry 8), the labor force (entry 15) and institutions and policies (entry 16) move it.

Check yourself

9.1 Frictional 2.5%, structural 1.5%. What is the natural rate?

Show answer

2.5 + 1.5 = 4%.

9.2 Is the natural rate the lowest unemployment an economy can ever reach?

Show answer

No. The actual rate can fall below it for a while, in a boom — entry 13.

10

Full employment and the natural rate

★ Exam

⚑ Not in this module — learn it from here. Your exam defines the natural rate as the unemployment rate when the economy produces its full-employment output. The phrase “full employment” does not appear anywhere in Module 13. You will need it from Unit 3 on.

Definition

The natural rate is the unemployment rate when the economy produces its full-employment real output. Full employment does not mean zero unemployment — it means unemployment equal to the natural rate.

Not in this module. Your textbook links output and the natural rate in Module 33.

Full-employment output (your exam also calls it potential output) is what the economy produces when unemployment is at its natural rate. At that output there is still frictional and structural unemployment, but no cyclical unemployment.

So the two ideas are one: output at full employment ↔ unemployment at the natural rate ↔ cyclical unemployment of zero. When output is below full employment, unemployment is above the natural rate; when output is above it, unemployment is below.

Watch out. “The economy is at full employment” and “unemployment is 5%” can both be true.

Textbook

Module 33, page 328. “When actual aggregate output is equal to potential output, the actual unemployment rate is equal to the natural rate of unemployment.”

Numeric

The diagram above. At the start the economy is at full employment, and unemployment is still 5%.

Illustrative

A 4.8% economy. If the natural rate is 4.8% and the actual rate is 4.8%, the economy is at full employment — with millions unemployed.

How it connects. It is the natural rate (entry 9) seen from the output side. Above or below it, the difference is cyclical unemployment (entry 11); entry 13 is the case where output is above it.

Check yourself

10.1 The economy is at full employment. How much cyclical unemployment is there?

Show answer

None. The actual rate equals the natural rate.

10.2 Unemployment is above the natural rate. Is output above or below full employment?

Show answer

Below. There is cyclical unemployment because output has fallen short.

11

Cyclical unemployment

★ Exam
Definition

The deviation of the actual unemployment rate from the natural rate: the part of unemployment caused by the business cycle.

Textbook page 130, margin.

When output falls in a recession, firms need fewer workers and lay people off across the whole economy. That extra unemployment, on top of the natural rate, is cyclical. When output recovers, it goes away.

It is the only type the cycle creates. Frictional and structural unemployment are there in good times and bad; cyclical unemployment is what a slump adds.

Watch out. A worker laid off in a recession whose job will come back is cyclically unemployed, however skilled she is.

Textbook

Melanie, part (c). Laid off in a slump in investment spending, with a promise to be rehired when business picks up. Cyclical.

Numeric

Your textbook’s figures. An actual rate of 9.7% in January 2010 against a natural rate of 4.8%: cyclical unemployment of about 4.9 points.

Illustrative

The diagram above. Press A recession: the actual rate rises to 8%, the natural rate stays at 5%, and cyclical unemployment is +3 points.

How it connects. It is measured from the natural rate (entry 9) and is zero at full employment (entry 10). It shrinks the frictional share (entry 4), the arithmetic is entry 12, it can be negative (entry 13), and entry 14 tells it from the other types.

Check yourself

11.1 Natural rate 5%, actual rate 7.5%. What is cyclical unemployment?

Show answer

7.5 − 5 = 2.5 points.

11.2 A car plant lays off workers because car sales have fallen nationwide in a recession. Which type?

Show answer

Cyclical. The jobs disappeared because output fell, and they return when it recovers.

12

Actual = natural + cyclical

★ Exam
Definition

Natural = frictional + structural. Actual = natural + cyclical. So cyclical = actual − natural.

Textbook page 130, equations 13-1 and 13-2.

Every calculation on this topic is one of these two lines. Given any three of frictional, structural, cyclical and actual, you can find the fourth.

Questions can give the parts as rates or as numbers of people. If they give people, divide by the labor force first, then add and subtract as usual.

Watch out. Subtract the natural rate from the actual rate, not the other way round. Get the sign wrong and you will call a boom a recession.

Textbook

Your AP Review, question 1. The rubric: the natural rate is frictional plus structural; cyclical is the difference between the actual and natural rates.

Numeric

Counting people. Labor force 160 million; 11.2 million unemployed of whom 4.8 million frictional and 3.2 million structural. Actual 7%, natural 5%, cyclical 2 points.

Illustrative

Working backwards. Actual 6.5%, cyclical 1.5 points, structural 2%. Natural rate = 5%; frictional = 3%.

How it connects. It is the definition of cyclical unemployment (entry 11) as arithmetic. When the answer comes out negative, read entry 13.

Check yourself

12.1 Frictional 3%, structural 1.5%, actual 7%. What is cyclical unemployment?

Show answer

Natural = 4.5%; cyclical = 7 − 4.5 = 2.5 points.

12.2 Labor force 50 million; 1.5 million frictional, 1 million structural, 3.5 million unemployed in all. Find the actual and natural rates.

Show answer

Actual 3.5 ÷ 50 = 7%; natural 2.5 ÷ 50 = 5%; cyclical 2 points.

13

When the actual rate is below the natural rate

★ Exam

⚑ Not in this module — learn it from here. Every example in Module 13 is a slump, with the actual rate above the natural rate. Your exam also asks about the opposite case, and its own sample free-response question starts there.

Definition

In a boom the actual rate can fall below the natural rate. Cyclical unemployment is then negative, and output is above its full-employment level.

Not in this module. Your textbook gives the rule in Module 33, page 328.

When demand is very strong, firms hire people they would normally pass over and workers find jobs faster than usual. Unemployment drops below its normal level. The subtraction actual − natural comes out negative.

It cannot last. Your textbook warns that holding unemployment below the natural rate leads to accelerating inflation, and Unit 3 shows the economy moving back. On the exam, write it as “unemployment is below the natural rate” and “output is above full employment”.

Watch out. A negative answer for cyclical unemployment is not a mistake. It tells you the economy is in a boom, not a recession.

Textbook

Module 33, page 328. “When the output gap is positive (an inflationary gap), the unemployment rate is below the natural rate.”

Numeric

Your exam board’s sample question. Actual rate 5%, natural rate 6%: cyclical unemployment is −1 point, so output is above full employment.

Illustrative

The diagram above. Press A boom: the actual rate is 4%, the natural rate 5%, and the dashed box is cyclical unemployment of −1 point.

How it connects. It is cyclical unemployment (entry 11) with the sign reversed, worked by entry 12, and the other side of full employment (entry 10).

Check yourself

13.1 Natural rate 5.5%, actual rate 4%. What is cyclical unemployment, and where is output?

Show answer

−1.5 points; output is above full employment.

13.2 Why can the actual rate not stay below the natural rate for long?

Show answer

Because it drives up wages and prices — accelerating inflation — and the economy moves back.

14

Telling the three types apart

★ Exam
Definition

Ask: Is the worker between jobs and searching? Frictional. Is there a lasting surplus of workers in their particular market? Structural. Did they lose the job because output fell? Cyclical.

Textbook pages 126–130 and Tackle the Test, free-response question 2.

The best single test: would this person still be unemployed if the economy were at full employment? If yes, the unemployment is frictional or structural. If no, it is cyclical.

Then separate the first two by asking whether more searching would solve it. A searcher with skills employers want is frictional. A worker whose skill, region or wage does not match any available job is structural.

Watch out. “Laid off” can be any of the three. Look for the reason: a move, a dying industry, or a slump.

Textbook

Melanie, three times. Laid off and turning down poor offers: frictional. Her industry moved the work abroad: structural. Laid off in a slump, rehire promised: cyclical.

Numeric

Your textbook’s review, question 1. Moved to a new state and took two months to find a job: frictional.

Illustrative

Bank tellers. Apps and ATMs replace most tellers’ work, and the jobs do not come back when the economy recovers. Structural.

How it connects. It sorts frictional (entry 3), structural (entry 5) and cyclical (entry 11) unemployment; seasonal unemployment (entry 17) goes with the first. The drill above practises it.

Check yourself

14.1 A construction worker loses his job when house-building collapses in a recession. Which type?

Show answer

Cyclical. The jobs vanished because output fell, and they return with the recovery.

14.2 A worker's factory moves overseas and no local firm needs his skills. Which type?

Show answer

Structural. More searching will not find a job that no longer exists in his market.

Drill: which type?

Twelve unemployed people. Put each in one of the three types. Ask first: would they still be unemployed if the economy were at full employment?

15

Changes in labor force characteristics

★ Exam
Definition

The natural rate changes when the labor force changes. Younger, less experienced workers change jobs more often and so have more frictional unemployment.

Textbook page 131, Figure 13.3.

Experienced workers stay in jobs longer, and older workers are more often family breadwinners with a strong reason to find and keep work. So a labor force with more young and new workers has a higher natural rate.

In the 1970s the baby boomers came of working age and many married women entered the paid labor force for the first time. The natural rate rose. By the late 1990s the labor force was more experienced, and the natural rate had fallen.

Watch out. A change in the labor force moves the natural rate. It is not cyclical unemployment, even if the actual rate rises too.

Textbook

January 2010. Overall unemployment was 9.7%; teenagers 26.4%; ages 20–24, 15.8%; ages 25–54, only 8.6%.

Numeric

The diagram above. Press A younger labor force: frictional rises to 3.8% and the natural rate to 5.8%.

Illustrative

An ageing country. Few young people are entering the labor force and most workers have been in their jobs for years. Its natural rate is likely to be low.

How it connects. It changes the natural rate (entry 9). The other causes are entry 16.

Check yourself

15.1 Why did the U.S. natural rate rise in the 1970s?

Show answer

A large influx of young, inexperienced workers — baby boomers — and of women entering the labor force for the first time.

15.2 A country's labor force becomes older and more experienced. What happens to its natural rate?

Show answer

It tends to fall, because frictional unemployment falls.

16

Changes in institutions and policies

★ Exam
Definition

Labor-market institutions and government policies move the natural rate too: unions, job-matching services, technology, benefits, job training and employment subsidies.

Textbook pages 131–132.

Things that raise it: strong unions, generous benefits, a high binding minimum wage, and new technology that cuts demand for unskilled workers when their wages cannot fall.

Things that lower it: temporary agencies and job websites, which shorten searches; job training, which widens the jobs a worker can do; and employment subsidies, which pay workers or employers to accept or offer jobs.

Watch out. A policy that lowers the natural rate makes full employment come with less unemployment. It does not create cyclical unemployment.

Textbook

The U.S. after 1980. A sharp fall in union membership may be one reason the natural rate fell between the 1970s and the 1990s.

Numeric

The diagram above. Press Job-matching websites: frictional falls to 2.5% and the natural rate to 4.5%.

Illustrative

A retraining scheme. Laid-off factory workers are taught to service wind turbines, where jobs are going unfilled. Structural unemployment falls.

How it connects. It moves the natural rate (entry 9), alongside entry 15. Benefits (entry 8) and the minimum wage (entry 6) are two of these policies.

Check yourself

16.1 Name one policy that lowers the natural rate, and say how.

Show answer

Job training: it gives the structurally unemployed skills for jobs that exist. (Or employment subsidies, or anything that speeds up matching.)

16.2 New technology raises demand for skilled workers and cuts it for unskilled ones, whose wage cannot fall because of a binding minimum wage. What happens?

Show answer

Structural unemployment rises, and so does the natural rate.

17

Seasonal unemployment

○ Background
Definition

Unemployment that comes back at the same time every year because some work is seasonal: a ski instructor in summer, a farm worker after the harvest.

Not in your textbook. Many review books use the term.

It is predictable and temporary, and the worker is between jobs and searching — so it behaves like frictional unemployment. Official figures are usually “seasonally adjusted” to take it out.

Your exam names only three types: frictional, structural and cyclical. If a question describes seasonal work, the answer it wants is almost always frictional.

Watch out. Do not answer “seasonal” when the options are frictional, structural and cyclical.

Textbook

Not in your textbook. Module 13 never uses the word “seasonal”; it treats every short spell between jobs as frictional.

Numeric

A beach town. Unemployment is 4% in July and 9% in January, the same every year — the extra five points are seasonal.

Illustrative

A department-store temp. Hired for the holiday rush and let go in January, she looks for her next job.

How it connects. It is a kind of frictional unemployment (entry 3), and entry 14 is how to classify it on the exam.

Check yourself

17.1 Is seasonal unemployment one of the three types your exam names?

Show answer

No. The three are frictional, structural and cyclical; seasonal behaves like frictional.

17.2 A lifeguard is out of work every winter and looks for indoor work. Which of the three types?

Show answer

Frictional.

Question 1 (7 points). (a) Define the natural rate of unemployment. (b) The natural rate of unemployment is made up of which of the types of unemployment? (c) Explain how cyclical unemployment relates to the natural rate of unemployment. (d) List three factors that can lead to a change in the natural rate of unemployment.

Show the answer and the rubric
  1. (a) The normal unemployment rate around which the actual unemployment rate fluctuates — one point
  2. (b) Frictional unemployment… — one point
  3. (b) …plus structural unemployment — one point
  4. (c) Cyclical unemployment is the deviation of the actual rate from the natural rate — one point
  5. (d) Changes in labor force characteristics — one point
  6. (d) Changes in labor market institutions, such as unions — one point
  7. (d) Changes in government policies — one point

For (a), your exam’s own wording is worth knowing too: the unemployment rate when the economy produces its full-employment output.

Question 2. In each situation, what type of unemployment is Melanie facing? Explain. (a) After completing a complex programming project, Melanie is laid off. Her prospects for a new job requiring similar skills are good, and she has signed up with a programmer placement service. She has passed up offers for low-paying jobs. (b) When Melanie and her co-workers refused to accept pay cuts, her employer outsourced their programming tasks to workers in another country. This is happening throughout the programming industry. (c) Because of a slump in investment spending, Melanie has been laid off. Her employer promises to rehire her when business picks up.

Show the answer

(a) Frictional. She is between jobs and searching for a good match.

(b) Structural. Wages in her market are above the level at which local jobs would exist, and the change is industry-wide and lasting.

(c) Cyclical. She lost the job because output fell, and it returns when output recovers.

Question 3 — how your exam board’s sample question begins. “The expected inflation rate is 4 percent, the actual rate of unemployment is 5 percent, the natural rate of unemployment is 6 percent…” (a) What is cyclical unemployment? (b) Is real output above, below or at its full-employment level?

Show the answer

(a) Cyclical = actual − natural = 5 − 6 = −1 point.

(b) Above. Unemployment is below the natural rate, so the economy is producing more than its full-employment output. The full question goes on to the AD–AS graph, which is Unit 3 — but reading these two numbers correctly is this module.

Frictional = searching between jobs. Structural = a lasting surplus of workers in a particular labor market. Cyclical = what a slump adds. Natural rate = frictional + structural = the rate at full employment. Cyclical = actual − natural, and it can be negative. The cycle moves the actual rate; the labor force, institutions and policy move the natural rate.

The five things to get right from Module 13

  1. Three types, and only three: frictional, structural, cyclical.
  2. Natural = frictional + structural. Cyclical is not part of it.
  3. Full employment is not zero unemployment. It is unemployment at the natural rate.
  4. Cyclical = actual − natural, and a negative answer means output is above full employment.
  5. To classify, ask: would this person still be unemployed at full employment? If not, it is cyclical.

The next two modules turn to inflation. In Unit 3 the natural rate becomes the vertical long-run aggregate supply line, and in Unit 5 it is where the long-run Phillips curve stands — and everything in entries 15 and 16 moves it.
